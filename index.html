Curcumin Same Switch, Different Symptoms

It switches off NF-κB — the master regulator behind chronic inflammation. Here's what that looks like across four conditions you see in clinic every week.

Three conditions, one shared inflammatory driver. Dysmenorrhea works through a separate pathway — see below. Tap a node to jump to the evidence.

Human RCT · 200 patients

PMOS (formerly PCOS)

The most common endocrine disorder in women of reproductive age — insulin resistance, dyslipidemia, and inflammation, all showing up together.

  • Improved cholesterol and blood glucose markers
  • Improved hormone levels — LH, FSH, testosterone
  • Greater effect than metformin or curcumin given alone

Feghhi F, et al. Phytomedicine, 2024. Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 12-week factorial trial.

Meta-analysis · 13 RCTs · 785 patients

Metabolic Syndrome

The metabolic load many PMOS patients are already carrying — elevated glucose, dyslipidemia, oxidative stress.

  • Waist circumference improved (p=0.009)
  • Fasting blood sugar improved (p=0.01)
  • Diastolic blood pressure improved (p=0.002)
  • HDL, the protective cholesterol, went up (p<0.0001)
  • CRP and TNF-α, both inflammation markers, went down (p<0.00001)

Qiu L, et al. Frontiers in Endocrinology, 2023. Systematic review and meta-analysis.

Human RCT · 240 patients · 9 months

Glycaemic Control

Insulin resistance sits upstream of both PMOS and metabolic syndrome. This trial asked a simple question: can curcumin stop prediabetes from becoming diabetes?

  • 0% of curcumin-treated patients progressed to type 2 diabetes over 9 months, vs. 16.4% on placebo
  • Insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) improved: 3.22 vs. 4.04, p<0.001
  • Beta-cell function (HOMA-β) improved: 61.58 vs. 48.72, p<0.01

Chuengsamarn S, et al. Diabetes Care, 2012. Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial.

Dysmenorrhea

A distinct mechanism, used differently — curcumin alongside an NSAID, not instead of one.

  • Turmeric combined with mefenamic acid relieved menstrual pain more than mefenamic acid alone

Hesami S, et al. J Gynecol Obstet Hum Reprod, 2021. Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial.
